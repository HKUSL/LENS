**Threat Model:**

- **Attack Assumption:**
  - The victim is an **HSS** exposing the **Cx** interface to the Internet.
  - The attacker can impersonate an **S-CSCF** and send a forged **Server-Assignment-Request (SAR)** to that HSS.
  - No IPsec/TLS or peer authentication is enforced.
  - The attacker knows the target subscriber's **Private User Identity** and can craft a protocol-plausible SAR for that user.

- **Attacker Capability:**
  - The attacker can open its own Diameter connection to the HSS, send SAR requests, and receive SAA responses.
  - The attacker cannot observe or alter traffic between the real S-CSCF and HSS.
  - The attacker is not itself a functioning IMS serving node, but under the threat model it can still submit syntactically and semantically plausible S-CSCF-originated SARs.

**Exploitability Analysis:**


According to 3GPP Cx procedures (TS 29.228 / 29.229 semantics):
- The **UAA** is sent by the **HSS** in response to a **UAR** from an I‑CSCF (or S‑CSCF).
- In UAA, the grouped **Server-Capabilities** AVP may be included and contains, among others:
  - **Mandatory-Capability** AVPs: numeric feature identifiers indicating capabilities that the serving S‑CSCF must support.
  - Optional-Capability, Server-Name, etc.
Thus, if the HSS accepts a UAR that triggers population of **Server-Capabilities**, the attacker will receive **Mandatory-Capability** values in the UAA.
The `Mandatory-Capability` AVPs advertise internal capabilities of the S‑CSCF (and related IMS core) that are required for serving this user or for a particular service profile. Examples (conceptually):
- Support for specific IMS/VoLTE features or 3GPP releases.
- Support for certain charging, QoS, or security extensions.
- Presence/absence of features such as specific header handling, routing behavior, or service triggers.
While these AVPs are not subscriber-personal data, they are **sensitive from a network security perspective** because they:
- Expose detailed **feature fingerprints** of core IMS elements (S‑CSCF types/versions/capabilities) that an external attacker should not normally see.
- Help an attacker:
  - Select or prioritize **protocol-level exploits** or malformed SIP/Diameter message patterns against specific implementations or features.
  - Infer **software version families** or vendor‑specific functionality from the advertised capability codes.
  - Tailor follow‑on attacks (e.g., bypassing weaker security options, targeting specific supplementary service implementations).
Therefore, learning `Server-Capabilities.Mandatory-Capability` via an unauthenticated Internet‑reachable Diameter Cx interface is **security-relevant** and aids reconnaissance and targeted exploitation.

**Attack Procedure:**

1. **Attacker role & impersonation**
   - The attacker impersonates the **I‑CSCF** using the provided attacker identity:
     - `Origin-Host = icscf1.ims.system.com`
     - `Origin-Realm = ims.system.com`

2. **Victim node**
   - The victim is the **HSS** Cx endpoint:
     - `Destination-Host = hss.ims.system.com`
     - `Destination-Realm = ims.system.com`

3. **Establish Diameter connection**
   - The attacker opens a TCP/SCTP connection to the HSS’s Diameter port and completes the ordinary Diameter capabilities exchange, spoofing the I‑CSCF identity.
   - Due to misconfiguration (no TLS/IPsec, weak peer validation), the HSS accepts this peer as a trusted I‑CSCF.

4. **Craft a single User-Authorization-Request (UAR)**
   - **Command-Code:** 300 (UAR/UAA)
   - **Application-Id:** use the provided `test_environment.diameter_common.application_id`:
     - `16777251`
   - **Flags:** Request (R) bit set; Proxiable (P) may also be set.
   - **Key AVPs to include:**
     - `Session-Id`: any unique string, e.g., `"icscf1.ims.system.com;1234567890"`.
     - `Auth-Session-State`: `NO_STATE_MAINTAINED (1)` to keep it simple.
     - `Origin-Host`: `icscf1.ims.system.com`
     - `Origin-Realm`: `ims.system.com`
     - `Destination-Realm`: `ims.system.com`
     - `Destination-Host`: `hss.ims.system.com`
     - `Vendor-Specific-Application-Id` containing:
       - `Vendor-Id`: `10415` (3GPP)
       - `Auth-Application-Id`: `16777251`
     - `User-Name`: `460000123456001@ims.mnc000.mcc460.3gppnetwork.org`
     - `Public-Identity`: `sip:460000123456001@ims.system.com`
     - `Visited-Network-Identifier`: `mcc000.mnc460.3gppnetwork.org`
     - `User-Authorization-Type`: `REGISTRATION (1)`
   - Under normal HSS behavior, this UAR will cause the HSS to perform user authorization for the given IMS Public Identity and return a UAA including the S‑CSCF information and corresponding **Server-Capabilities** for this subscriber.

5. **Receive and inspect the UAA**
   - The attacker passively receives the single **User-Authorization-Answer (UAA)**.
   - Inside the UAA, the attacker inspects:
     - `Server-Capabilities` grouped AVP.
       - Extracts all `Mandatory-Capability` AVPs and records their integer values.
   - No further interaction is required; the attack succeeds within this one-shot exchange.
