**Threat Model:**

- **Attack Assumption:**
  - On S6a, `Cancel-Location-Request (CLR)` is sent from **HSS to MME**. Under the fixed threat model, the attacker impersonates an **HSS** and sends a forged but protocol-plausible CLR to a publicly exposed **MME** over an unauthenticated Diameter connection.
  - The attacker knows the target subscriber IMSI and the Diameter identity of the target MME.
  - The MME accepts the CLR and processes `Cancellation-Type` as defined in TS 29.272.

- **Attacker Capability:**
  - The attacker can open its own Diameter connection to the victim MME, send crafted CLR requests, and receive the corresponding CLA answers.
  - The attacker cannot interfere with legitimate HSS<->MME traffic, cannot act as a real serving node, and has no radio or bearer-plane control.
  - The attack is limited to effects the MME will perform locally or trigger downstream as a consequence of processing the forged CLR.

**Exploitability Analysis:**

`Cancellation-Type` is a **mandatory top-level Enumerated AVP** in CLR on S6a. It is processed directly by the **receiving MME** and is not mere metadata. Per the supplied protocol evidence, TS 29.272 requires the MME, after identifying the subscriber via `User-Name`, to **check `Cancellation-Type` and act accordingly**.

This makes the field independently security-relevant. The enclosing CLR procedure is already sensitive, but the **specific value** of `Cancellation-Type` determines which destructive branch the MME executes:

- `SUBSCRIPTION_WITHDRAWAL (2)` causes the MME to **delete subscription data** and **detach the UE**. Per TS 23.401, this can lead to **NAS Detach Request**, paging if needed, and teardown of active session state.
- `INITIAL_ATTACH_PROCEDURE (4)` explicitly **does not delete subscription data**, but can clear SMS registration state and drive old-context cleanup.
- `MME_UPDATE_PROCEDURE (0)` and related update causes drive **old serving/MM/bearer context removal** and release of old Serving GW resources.

So the field is not simply describing why a generic cancellation happened; it is the **authoritative selector of the receiver's cleanup/detach behavior**. Changing only this AVP changes whether the MME performs:
- full detach plus subscription-data deletion,
- narrower mobility cleanup,
- or SMS-registration state changes without subscription deletion.

That is a distinct, field-attributable security effect.

The strongest field-specific abuse under the threat model is to set:

- `Cancellation-Type = SUBSCRIPTION_WITHDRAWAL (2)`

Because that value selects the most disruptive standards-defined path: deletion of subscription data in the MME and UE detach. This is broader and more persistent than the update or initial-attach variants. The resulting denial of service persists until later legitimate attach/location-update procedures rebuild state, and recovery is not immediate.

This satisfies the exploitability threshold because:
1. the attacker can send a forged CLR under the fixed model;
2. the MME normatively processes `Cancellation-Type`;
3. the value alters stored per-subscriber state and later behavior;
4. the resulting detach/subscription-data deletion is concrete, harmful, and specifically caused by the chosen field value.

**Attack Procedure:**

1. Establish a Diameter connection to the exposed MME while impersonating the HSS role.
2. Send a minimal but coherent **CLR request** for a subscriber IMSI currently known to that MME.
3. Include only mandatory and coherence-related AVPs:
   - `Session-Id`
   - `Auth-Session-State`
   - `Origin-Host`
   - `Origin-Realm`
   - `Destination-Host`
   - `Destination-Realm`
   - `User-Name`
   - `Cancellation-Type`
   - optionally `Vendor-Specific-Application-Id` for plausibility
4. Set the target field:
   - `Cancellation-Type = SUBSCRIPTION_WITHDRAWAL (2)`
5. Expected success condition:
   - the MME accepts the CLR and returns a successful CLA;
   - if the IMSI is known, the MME processes the subscription-withdrawal branch, deleting subscriber data from the MME context and detaching the UE, with possible downstream NAS detach and session teardown behavior.

Only mandatory/coherence-related fields are included below; unrelated optional AVPs are excluded.
