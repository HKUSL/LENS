### **Threat Model:**
- **Attack Assumption:**
  - The victim is an **S-CSCF** exposing its **Cx Diameter** service due to misconfiguration. An Internet attacker can impersonate the **HSS** role and establish an unauthenticated Diameter connection to that S-CSCF. The victim accepts forged **Registration-Termination-Request (RTR)** messages and processes them as authoritative HSS-originated registration-control commands.

- **Attacker Capability:**
  - The attacker can actively send crafted **RTR** requests on its own Diameter connection and receive the resulting **RTA** answers. The attacker does not observe or alter legitimate HSS-S-CSCF traffic and has no extra SIP/radio/EPC powers beyond supplying a valid target subscriber identity and syntactically correct Cx AVPs, including `Deregistration-Reason.Reason-Code`.

### **Exploitability Analysis:**
`Deregistration-Reason.Reason-Code` is a **nested enumerated control AVP** inside `Deregistration-Reason` in an **RTR request** from HSS to S-CSCF. In this context it is not passive metadata: the S-CSCF uses it as **authoritative input** to classify the deregistration being ordered.


Changing `Reason-Code` can influence **how** the S-CSCF performs cleanup-e.g., treating the event as `PERMANENT_TERMINATION` versus `SERVER_CHANGE`/`NEW_SERVER_ASSIGNED`-but the evidence only supports differences in local deregistration semantics, expected reassignment behavior, or degree of purge. It does not show that this field alone drives a distinct downstream exploit such as persistent subscriber-data corruption, EPC bearer/control changes, wrong APN/QoS, authorization escalation, or a materially different network impact that would not already arise from the forged RTR.
**Attack Procedure:**
1. Establish a Diameter connection to the target S-CSCF while impersonating an HSS peer.
2. Craft a minimal but coherent `Registration-Termination-Request (RTR)` with mandatory AVPs only, plus a `Public-Identity` to precisely target one IMS public identity.
3. Set the target field `Deregistration-Reason.Reason-Code` to a malicious test value. A strong field-specific test is `PERMANENT_TERMINATION (0)` because it exercises the strongest de-registration semantic and the least self-healing recovery path.
4. Keep identity targeting constant across tests; vary only `Reason-Code` if comparing field-specific behavior.
5. Send the RTR and observe successful acceptance via the corresponding RTA plus subscriber-impacting behavior such as IMS deregistration, session disruption, or failed/changed re-registration path.

Only mandatory and coherence-related AVPs are included below; unrelated AVPs are excluded.
