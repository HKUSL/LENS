### **Threat Model:**
- **Attack Assumption:**
  - On S6a, the normal sender of an `Insert-Subscriber-Data-Request` is the **HSS** and the victim receiver is the **MME**. We assume the MME is misconfigured so an Internet attacker can impersonate the HSS, open a Diameter connection to the MME, and send forged IDRs that the MME accepts as valid subscriber-data updates.

- **Attacker Capability:**
  - The attacker can actively send crafted `Insert-Subscriber-Data-Request` messages on its own Diameter connection and receive the corresponding answers. The attacker knows the target subscriber identity needed for `User-Name`, but cannot observe or alter legitimate HSS<->MME traffic and has no extra NAS/GTP/PGW control beyond what follows from the MME accepting the forged S6a request.

### **Exploitability Analysis:**
- `Subscription-Data.APN-Configuration-Profile.APN-Configuration.Served-Party-IP-Address` is a 3GPP vendor-specific **Address** AVP carried as authoritative HSS provisioning inside the subscriber's APN profile. It is not mere metadata about the sender; it is subscriber state that the MME stores under a specific `APN-Configuration`, typically keyed by `Context-Identifier` and associated `Service-Selection`.

- Under the threat model, changing this field can poison the MME's cached APN subscription state for the targeted subscriber by replacing the APN-specific provisioned IP address with an attacker-chosen IPv4 or IPv6 value. The field is optional, but when present it has provisioning semantics: it tells the MME that this APN context is associated with that served-party address.

- The field-specific causal chain is:
  1. The attacker sends a forged IDR from the spoofed HSS role to the MME.
  2. Inside `Subscription-Data`, the attacker includes an `APN-Configuration-Profile` entry for the victim subscriber and sets `Served-Party-IP-Address` in the chosen `APN-Configuration`.
  3. The MME updates/stores that APN subscription entry as HSS-authoritative profile data.
  4. On a later attach or PDN Connectivity procedure for that APN, the MME reuses the poisoned APN configuration when handling PDN establishment logic.
  5. If the provisioned address is inconsistent with `PDN-Type`, stale, unauthorized, or conflicts with PGW-side policy, the resulting session setup can fail or behave incorrectly for that APN.

- This is a real **field-specific** control effect because the negative outcome depends on the value of this leaf AVP itself: the MME's stored subscriber APN profile is changed in an address-specific way. The most credible impact is **subscriber data corruption leading to later PDN/bearer establishment mismatch or failure** for the affected APN.

- The impact is narrower than changing APN selection or QoS AVPs, and successful realization of a wrong static IP assignment may still depend on PGW behavior. But the evidence supports a concrete exploit path even without PGW compromise: poisoning this field can leave the MME with inconsistent APN address state that is later consumed during session handling, producing service disruption for that subscriber. That is enough for field-specific exploitability under the stated rules.

- The effect is usually not immediate at IDR receipt; it is typically realized on a later PDN establishment or related reevaluation. The poisoned value is persistent within the UE's MME subscriber context until overwritten by a later legitimate IDR, a fresh subscription download, or context teardown/re-registration.

### **Attack Procedure:**
- Craft a forged `Insert-Subscriber-Data-Request` on S6a from the spoofed HSS identity to the target MME, using the victim subscriber's `User-Name`.
- Include `Subscription-Data` -> `APN-Configuration-Profile` -> one `APN-Configuration` with:
  - `Context-Identifier`
  - `Service-Selection`
  - `PDN-Type`
  - `Served-Party-IP-Address` set to a malicious or inconsistent static IP value
- The goal is to make the MME store the modified APN profile entry for that subscriber.
- Expected success condition: the MME accepts the IDR, and the subscriber later experiences failed or altered PDN establishment for the targeted APN due to the poisoned served-party IP address.
