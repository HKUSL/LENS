### **Threat Model:**
- **Attack Assumption:**
  - The victim is the `MME` on the `S6a` interface. An Internet attacker can impersonate the `HSS` and send a forged `Insert-Subscriber-Data-Request (IDR)` directly to that `MME`, which accepts the request as trusted subscriber-profile input for the targeted UE.

- **Attacker Capability:**
  - The attacker can establish its own unauthenticated Diameter connection to the `MME`, send crafted `IDR` requests, and receive the corresponding answers. The attacker knows the subscriber identity needed to target the UE, but cannot observe or modify legitimate `HSS`-`MME` traffic and has no extra EPC, radio, or policy-node control beyond the forged `S6a` request itself.

### **Exploitability Analysis:**
- `Subscription-Data.AMBR.Max-Requested-Bandwidth-DL` is authoritative subscriber provisioning data inside `IDR`, not descriptive metadata. It sits under `Subscription-Data -> AMBR` and is interpreted by the `MME` as the subscribed downlink aggregate maximum bit rate for non-GBR service.
- If a forged `IDR` is accepted, the `MME` updates the UE's stored subscription context with the attacker-supplied `AMBR` values. Changing this specific leaf AVP changes the downlink component of that stored policy state, while leaving the rest of the procedure unchanged.
- The field-specific causal chain is concrete: forged `IDR` -> `MME` stores altered DL `AMBR` -> later bearer/PDN/session procedures reuse the updated subscribed AMBR -> downstream EPC enforcement or signaling applies the reduced or inflated aggregate DL limit -> the subscriber experiences throttling, degraded non-GBR throughput, or altered QoS treatment.
- This is field-specific exploitability because the harmful effect depends on the value of `Max-Requested-Bandwidth-DL` itself. A forged `IDR` is broadly dangerous, but this field independently controls one particular subscriber policy parameter: the downlink aggregate bitrate ceiling.
- The effect is not merely hypothetical sender-claimed capability. The attacker does not need extra powers outside the threat model; once the `MME` accepts the forged provisioning update, legitimate later EPC procedures can consume the poisoned value.
- The impact is usually persistent within the serving `MME`'s UE context until detach, purge, restart, a fresh attach/TAU subscription download, or a later legitimate `IDR` overwrites it. That overwrite path limits duration but does not negate the concrete field-driven service impact.

### **Attack Procedure:**
- Craft a forged `S6a` `Insert-Subscriber-Data-Request` from the fake `HSS` to the target `MME` with the targeted subscriber identity and a `Subscription-Data` grouped AVP containing `AMBR`.
- Inside `AMBR`, include both `Max-Requested-Bandwidth-UL` and `Max-Requested-Bandwidth-DL`, setting the target field to a maliciously low downlink value such as `1` or another throttling value that is syntactically valid.
- Keep the rest of the request minimal but valid: include standard `IDR` routing/session AVPs and the required grouped ancestors so the `MME` can bind the update to the active UE context.
- Observable success condition: the `MME` returns a successful `IDA`, stores the modified subscription data, and the subscriber later experiences reduced non-GBR downlink throughput when the updated AMBR is used in bearer/session handling.
