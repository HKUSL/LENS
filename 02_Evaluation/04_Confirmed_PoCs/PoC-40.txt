### **Threat Model:**
- **Attack Assumption:**
  - The victim is an **MME** exposing its **S6a Diameter** service. An Internet attacker impersonates the **HSS** and sends a forged **Insert-Subscriber-Data-Request (IDR)** to that MME. The target field is `Subscription-Data.APN-Configuration-Profile.APN-Configuration.AMBR.Max-Requested-Bandwidth-UL`, i.e., the uplink component of APN-AMBR inside a subscriber's APN profile.

- **Attacker Capability:**
  - The attacker can open its own Diameter connection to the MME, send syntactically valid forged **Requests**, and receive the corresponding **Answers**. The attacker does not observe or alter real HSS-MME traffic and has no extra NAS/GTP/radio capabilities. If subscriber targeting is needed, the attacker is assumed to know the subscriber identity and enough APN-profile context to identify the intended `APN-Configuration` instance.

### **Exploitability Analysis:**
`Max-Requested-Bandwidth-UL` is **field-specifically exploitable** under this threat model because it is not mere metadata: it is authoritative **subscriber provisioning input** inside IDR, and the MME uses it to update stored APN subscription state for a specific subscriber and APN configuration.

The causal chain is concrete. A forged IDR from the spoofed HSS carries `Subscription-Data` with an `APN-Configuration-Profile`, a chosen `APN-Configuration` instance, and an `AMBR` group containing a malicious `Max-Requested-Bandwidth-UL` value. If accepted, the MME stores that value in the subscriber's APN profile cache as the uplink APN-AMBR for that APN. This is per-subscriber and per-APN state, not just per-transaction metadata.

Changing this field specifically changes the uplink aggregate bandwidth limit associated with that APN. A very low value can cause severe uplink service degradation or effective denial of service for traffic using that APN once the updated subscription data is applied in later PDN/bearer handling. A very high value can also alter policy/QoS treatment if downstream EPC behavior honors the modified profile. The key point is that the negative outcome is driven by this field's value itself: it changes the rate limit parameter the MME later uses or propagates, rather than merely triggering generic IDR processing.

The effect may not always be instant for already-active sessions, but that does not defeat exploitability. The evidence shows the field is stored and later reused for PDN connectivity/bearer handling, so the attacker can poison subscriber state that will affect subsequent or refreshed EPC procedures without needing additional non-Diameter capabilities.

This is therefore not merely "the forged IDR is bad anyway." The incremental harm here is that this particular field lets the attacker set a concrete APN-level uplink QoS limit and thereby induce targeted subscriber service degradation.

### **Attack Procedure:**
Craft a forged **Insert-Subscriber-Data-Request** from the attacker-controlled Diameter connection to the MME, using the target subscriber identity and a coherent `Subscription-Data` payload. Inside `APN-Configuration-Profile`, include the intended `APN-Configuration` identified by `Context-Identifier` and `Service-Selection`, then include `AMBR` with a maliciously low `Max-Requested-Bandwidth-UL` and a matching `Max-Requested-Bandwidth-DL` for semantic coherence.

A practical malicious setting is an extremely low uplink value, causing the subscriber's APN profile to be updated with a near-zero uplink APN-AMBR. Success is indicated by a successful IDA/answer from the MME and subsequent subscriber behavior showing reduced or unusable uplink throughput on the affected APN during later or refreshed PDN/bearer handling.
